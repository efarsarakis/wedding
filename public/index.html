Getting there

Travel to Crete

Getting to Crete Heraklion (HER)

Heraklion is your airport — Lentas sits about 75 km south, roughly an hour and twenty minutes' drive through the Asterousia mountains. Chania (CHQ) is technically an option but adds three hours of driving; don't.

  • easyJet and Jet2 both fly direct to Heraklion through the summer from a good spread of UK airports. Routes shift from year to year, so check both for whichever airport suits you.
  • September 2027 is on sale now with both airlines, so you can book whenever you're ready. easyJet is probably the more convenient, with a Saturday flight to Heraklion; Jet2 has the more generous hand-baggage allowance. Checked luggage is an extra on both, so compare the total rather than the headline fare.
  • Year-round connections via Athens (ATH) with Aegean or SKY Express — under an hour for the hop. There are also ferries from Athens (Piraeus), and to/from many of the Aegean islands.

Once you land

Hiring a car is the way — Lentas is a beautifully remote fishing village and the drive down (via Agioi Deka) is half the experience. Book ahead; UK licences are accepted.

Prefer not to drive? Once you're in Lentas, everything is on foot. Taxis to and from Lentas can be arranged, but it's remote enough that they're more faff than they're usually worth; if you're not hiring a car, try to team up with someone who is — and if you can't find anyone, tell us and we'll do our best to help.

Flying from Edinburgh on the Saturday? If enough of you land together, we plan to hire a minibus — on us — to take you straight to your accommodation in Lentas. We may also be able to help arrange the trip back to the airport if people are leaving together — just let us know.

Practicalities

Passport
UK visitors don't need a visa, and there's nothing to worry about at the border. By 2027 you may need an ETIAS — a cheap online travel permit, much like the US ESTA — so check before you fly.
Currency
Euro — cards are accepted in most places, but Lentas venues generally favour cash. It's a laid-back sort of place.
Language
Greek; English widely spoken. "Yamas!" (cheers) will get you far
Weather
Early September: 26–29 °C, sunny, warm evenings — and the sea at its warmest all year
Plugs
Type C/F (European two-pin) — bring an adapter

Where to stay

Accommodation

Lentas is laid-back and a little bohemian — the kind of village where nobody hurries and the day is measured in swims. Don't come expecting five-star hotels: this is rooms-to-let country, simple and family-run, with a charmingly old-school, slightly frozen-in-time feel. Think sea breeze and simple comforts rather than spa robes and room service.

Our island

Explore Crete

The queen's stone menagerie

Find all three petrified guardians: the lion headland above the village, the elephant rock on the coastal path east, and the crocodile of Cape Trachoulas beyond. Below the lion lie the ruins of ancient Lebena and its healing sanctuary of Asclepius.

Matala

The famous bay where the hippies lived in the caves in the sixties — under an hour away.

The beaches

Agiofarago, Trachoulas, Komos and Diskos (Ditikos) — most of them remote, and several of them naturist-friendly ;)

Heraklion & Knossos

The archaeological museum and the palace of the Minotaur's labyrinth — a good day out, or a stop on the way home.

On foot

Crete is criss-crossed with walking paths, from easy coastal strolls to proper mountain and gorge hikes. The path east towards Tripiti starts right from the village.

Further afield

We've stuck to what's within easy reach of Lentas. The rest of the island — Chania, Rethymno, the Samaria Gorge, the far east — is well worth it, but more than a day trip. Save it for a longer stay.

Good questions

FAQ

What should I wear?

Summer fabulous. Linen, colour, a good hat, the shirt you've been saving — dress like the photographs will be looked at for fifty years, but stay cool enough to dance at midnight. The ceremony is outdoors on stone above the Libyan Sea, so choose footwear accordingly. Kilts enthusiastically welcomed; you will probably not be the only one.

Why a Monday?

Because the venue is ours for the whole week, and a Monday wedding lets you fly in on the Saturday, settle for a day, and still have the rest of the week ahead of you rather than behind you. Take the Monday and Tuesday off; you'll thank us.

How hot will it be, really?

Early September days reach 26–29 °C with strong sun; evenings settle to a lovely 21–23 °C. The ceremony runs in the evening for exactly this reason. The sea is at its warmest all year — bring swimmers (or don't ;) ).

Do I need to come to all three days?

Not at all. The Monday is the heart of it; Sunday and Tuesday are there so that a long journey buys more than one evening together.

Can I bring a plus one?

Your invitation names everyone in your party. If your circumstances have changed, just write to us — we'll do our best.

Are children welcome?

It's an adults' evening at heart — a late ceremony, a long dinner and a very late party. Children aren't banned: if yours truly can't be left, they're welcome and we'll make sure they're fed and looked after. But if you can arrange for them to stay home, we'd gently encourage it — and you'll enjoy the raki more.

I don't speak Greek — will I manage?

Completely. English is spoken everywhere tourists go in Crete, menus are bilingual, and half the wedding party will happily translate the other half's speeches.

What if flights or plans fall through?

Things happen, especially with international travel. Tell us as soon as you know and we'll adjust — no guilt, ever. Travel insurance is strongly recommended when you book.

Who do I contact on the day?

We'll have someone coordinating on the day, and their name and number will be here with the invitations. Please don't call the grooms — they'll be busy panicking about cufflinks.
